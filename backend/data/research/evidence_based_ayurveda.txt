TITLE: Evidence-Based Ayurveda
TYPE: Research-methodology and evidence-literacy document

WHY EVIDENCE MATTERS
Ayurveda contains a large body of traditional knowledge. When making health claims, it is important to distinguish historical/traditional use from evidence generated through modern research.

EVIDENCE LEVELS
1. Traditional knowledge and historical texts:
   Useful for understanding how a practice or substance has been used traditionally. They do not by themselves establish modern clinical effectiveness or safety for every person.

2. Laboratory / in-vitro research:
   Can investigate biological mechanisms or chemical activity. Results from cells or laboratory systems cannot automatically be translated into clinical benefit in humans.

3. Animal studies:
   Can provide preliminary information about mechanisms, toxicity, and potential effects. Animal findings do not establish effectiveness in humans.

4. Observational studies:
   Can identify associations in real-world populations but are vulnerable to confounding and other biases. Association does not necessarily mean causation.

5. Randomized controlled trials:
   Can provide stronger evidence about causal effects under specified conditions, but results still depend on study quality, sample size, population, intervention, comparator, and outcomes.

6. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses:
   Can synthesize multiple studies. Their reliability depends on the quality and comparability of the included studies.

IMPORTANT PRINCIPLES
- A positive laboratory result does not prove a clinical treatment works.
- A single small study does not establish a universal medical claim.
- Statistical significance is not the same as clinical importance.
- Evidence may apply only to a specific preparation, dose, population, and duration.
- Different commercial herbal products may not be equivalent.
- Safety evidence is separate from effectiveness evidence.

CHATBOT ANSWERING RULE
For each health claim, prefer this structure:
A. What Ayurveda traditionally says.
B. What research has studied.
C. What the evidence actually supports.
D. What remains uncertain.
E. Relevant safety considerations.

Do not manufacture citations or evidence. If the uploaded knowledge base does not contain enough evidence, say that the available sources are insufficient.
